Why we're doing this

Today AMG has two separate apps. One is the older staff back office, called the Portal. The other is the newer app that families see, called the OS. They have different web addresses, different logins, and different looks, and they don't share information.

Both apps have good pieces. Both are also cluttered with test entries, duplicate alerts, broken screens, and false alarms. You've told us you want the experience to be simple for the families you serve. This phase cleans the house and designs that simple version, so the full build starts from solid ground.

What you get

Week 1

A. We take the keys

We move both apps, and the accounts they run on, into AOM's care. AMG stays the owner of everything. Nothing goes offline while we do this. You get a one-page list of every account and service the apps depend on, and who holds each one.

Weeks 1–3

B. A clean house

  • Test clutter removed. Every test account, sample entry, and duplicate is cleared from the screens your team and families see.
  • The dashboard stops showing false red. The copied-and-pasted alerts that make everything look like a crisis are cleared, and the cause is fixed.
  • Broken screens fixed. This covers the empty task board, report emails that never send, duplicated message templates, the "tamper detected" warning, the dead help link, and the unfinished assessment list.
  • Email and text connections sorted. Each one is either reconnected and working, or removed from the screen so nobody trips over it.
Weeks 2–4

C. The alarms get quiet

Right now the family's news feed flags hundreds of stories as urgent. Most have nothing to do with the family. One example: a sports-show host who shares the family's last name. We fix it so that only news about the family's actual people, places, and trips is marked urgent. Repeated copies of the same story are removed.

What you'll see: a family's alert list goes from hundreds of "HIGH" flags to a short list a person would actually read.

Week 2

D. Documents kept safe

Files uploaded to the family's document vault currently sit on temporary storage and could disappear. We move them to permanent, encrypted, backed-up storage before anyone uploads something real.

What you get continued

Weeks 2–3

E. Screen-by-screen walkthrough with your team

Two working sessions, about 90 minutes each. We go through every screen in both apps together and decide what to keep, what to cut, and what to combine. You come out with a written list of those decisions.

Weeks 3–5

F. The One App design

A clickable mockup of the simplified app. You can click through it like the real thing. It shows:

  • what a family sees the moment they log in,
  • what the AMG team sees,
  • what an outside partner sees,
  • one login and one look for all three.

Includes two rounds of changes based on your feedback.

Week 5

G. The plan and a fixed price for the full build

A written schedule and a fixed price for building the One App (Phase 2), based on what we found. You decide whether to continue. There is no obligation to go further.

Ground rules

  1. AMG owns it. Everything we make for AMG, including the design, the fixes, and the documents, belongs to AMG once it's paid for. AOM keeps its general know-how and tools, but not AMG's content.
  2. Confidentiality. Anything we see about AMG, its families, its partners, or its pricing stays private. It is used only for this project and not shared, during the project or after.
  3. Access handled carefully. We only ask for the access we need. Passwords are kept in a secure password manager, never in email. When the engagement ends, all access is handed back.
  4. Inherited work. The existing apps were built by others. We'll fix what's listed here and flag anything else we find. We aren't responsible for problems we didn't create, or for problems outside this list, unless we agree in writing to take them on.
  5. Changes. If AMG wants something not listed here, we'll describe it and price it in writing before doing it. Nothing extra gets billed without a written yes.
  6. Not a security service. The software helps AMG's team spot and organize risks. It does not replace AMG's professional judgment or the partners who provide protection. AOM is not responsible for decisions made using the app's information.
  7. Liability. AOM's total responsibility under this agreement is capped at the fees AMG has paid under it.
  8. Walking away. Either side can end this agreement with 10 days' written notice. AMG pays for work done up to that point and receives everything completed so far.
  9. Governing law. This agreement is governed by the laws of the State of Arizona.
