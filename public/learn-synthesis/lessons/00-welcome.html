Module 00 · Getting started

Welcome: your MPC is an instrument builder

The MPC is a sampler and drum machine. This course teaches you something bigger: how to build instruments from the ground up. By the end, you will have written 8 sounds of your own.

The idea

Your MPC becomes a build-your-own-instrument workshop

A normal synth hands you preset sounds. A keygroup track hands you a workshop. The thing you build in it is called a keygroup: one sound you design from scratch, its tone, its filter, the way it swells and fades. You play it with your keyboard. Press a low key and it sounds low; a high key, high. It is a real, playable instrument spread across the keys.

Here is the part that makes it powerful. One track can hold many keygroups, and you give each one a range of keys. Put a bass on the low keys and a lead up top and you have split the keyboard. Stack two keygroups on the same keys and they layer into one thicker sound. Spread a whole set out, each covering a few notes with its own sample, and you have built something big like a piano. Range is the control surface, not a pad. Advanced mode is the engine that makes all of this; leave Legacy off.

On the MPC · Global tab

What you are looking at

Make a new track and set its type to Keygroup with Advanced on. Now the keys play your keygroup. The 16 pads mirror a stretch of those same notes, pad by pad, note by note, so you can finger-drum a part too. A pad is not a separate instrument; it is just another way to play a note. When you are building, tapping a pad or pressing a key is how you pick which keygroup you are shaping.

Hear it · live in your browser

Hear four keygroups you'll learn to build

Each button plays a different keygroup: a deep bass, a soft pad, a plucky lead, a hat. Four separate sounds, all made from the same engine. On your MPC you would play each one across the keys; here, just tap to preview the kind of thing you're about to build.

Build it with me · make your first keygroup

Five steps to a working track

  1. Make a new trackPress the button to add a track. Any track will do.
  2. Set its type to KeygroupOn the Global tab, find the dropdown that says "Drum Track" or "Keyboard Track." Change it to "Keygroup."
  3. Check that Advanced is onOn the same Global tab, look for a toggle that says "Advanced" or "Legacy." Make sure Advanced is on. Legacy stays off.
  4. You've got your first keygroupThe keys now play it, low to high. Right now it is empty, waiting for a sound.
  5. Give it a sound, then a rangeAdd layers (a sample or an oscillator), then a filter, envelopes, and modulation until it is a finished sound. Want a second instrument in the same track? Add another keygroup and set its key range so the two sit side by side (a split) or stack on the same keys (a layer).
