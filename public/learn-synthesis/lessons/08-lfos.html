Module 08 · The machine

LFOs: automatic movement

An LFO is an invisible hand that wiggles a knob over and over. By the end you will understand how they work, hear one move a sound in real time, and build a wobbling pad that evolves on its own.

The idea

An invisible hand on a knob

Imagine someone is sitting at your synth and they reach over and wiggle a knob back and forth. Slow wiggles. Over and over. That is an LFO.

LFO stands for Low Frequency Oscillator. It is not a sound itself. It is a slow wave that controls a different knob. You pick which knob. That knob could be Pitch (making the sound vibrate up and down). It could be Filter (making the sound wobble). It could be Volume (making the sound pulse on and off).

The only rule is this: the LFO wiggles invisible, but you hear the result on the sound. That is the magic. You get movement without playing anything new.

On the MPC · LFO tabs

Hear it · live in your browser

Choose a destination and listen

Press play. A pad is humming. Now choose Pitch (you hear vibrato, like a voice shaking), Filter (you hear the tone get bright and dark), or Amp (you hear the volume pulsing). Feel the difference in each one. Drag Rate slow and fast to hear how speed changes the feel.

Sound is generated live, no files. Works best with headphones or speakers up.

Build it with me · make a wobbling pad

Five steps to your own LFO pad

  1. Make a keygroup trackNew track, set its type to Keygroup. This is the instrument-builder mode.
  2. Set a layer to Wavetable oscillatorOn the Samples/Oscs tab, switch Layer 1 to Oscillator, then pick Wavetable. Wavetable works great with LFOs because it is already interesting.
  3. Make the pad swellOn the Amp envelope, raise Attack and Release. Long attack means the sound fades in like a breath.
  4. Add a slow filter wobbleIn the LFO 1 tab, set Wave to Sine, set Rate slow (3 to 5 Hz), and turn on To Filter 1. The tone will breathe.
  5. Stir in a low-pass filterDrop a gentle low-pass filter and let it open. Play a chord, hold it, listen to the LFO wiggle the tone.
