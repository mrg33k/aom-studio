Module 17 · Build lab

Build a Hip Hop kit

Hip hop is bass, drums, and attitude. You will build a sub-bass oscillator with a pitch drop, craft a wavetable stab, program mute-group hats, and combine them into a groove that hits hard.

The idea

Heavy bass meets sharp stabs

Hip hop drums are about impact and pocket. The kick is sub bass. a sine wave that starts where humans feel it instead of hearing it. The stab is quick and sharp. a synth sound that punctures the beat. The hat is clicky and controlled. using mute groups so open and closed versions never play at once.

The secret: a tiny dip in pitch on the 808 at the very start (called a pitch envelope) makes it sound like a real drum being hit. The wavetable stab gets its attitude from a filter that opens fast. The hats sit in a mute group so tapping closed kills the open one.

That pocket feeling comes from timing, velocity, and the sounds having room to breathe. Enough decay so they don't cut off abruptly.

On the keys: keep the 808 as its own keygroup on the low keys in Mono, and put your stabs or leads up top. One track, split by range.

On the MPC · Samples/Oscs tab

The hip hop layer stack

Each keygroup pad holds up to 8 layers. A classic hip hop setup stacks them like this: Layer 1 is the 808 sub (Warm Sine with FM for growl). Layer 2 is the stab (Wavetable or Saw). Layer 3 is a noise layer for grit. You choose which to trigger by velocity or by tapping different pads in the same keygroup, or you can layer all of them to play at once.

Hear it · live in your browser

Tap the pads and play the pattern

Press each pad to hear it. The 808 drops in pitch at the attack. The stab is bright and punchy. The hat is filtered noise. Hit Play Pattern to hear them lock together into a groove. The 808 and stab hit on the downbeat, hats trade in between. That is the pocket.

Sound is generated live, no files. Tap pads or press the pattern button. Works best with headphones or speakers up.

Build it with me · kit from scratch

Six steps to your hip hop kit

  1. Make a new keygroup trackCreate a track, set it to Keygroup type. This is your sound-building zone.
  2. Add Layer 1: Warm Sine for 808 subSet Layer 1 to Oscillator, pick Warm Sine. On the Pitch envelope, add a fast decay so pitch drops at the attack. That is the 808 character.
  3. Add Layer 2: Wavetable stabAdd a second layer, set it to Oscillator, pick Wavetable. Keep it high in pitch, bright position value. On the Amp envelope, make attack very fast and release short. Stabs don't linger.
  4. Add Layer 3: Noise for gritAdd a third layer, set it to Noise. Lower the level so it is a texture under the stab, not the star. Shape it with a low-pass filter if you want it softer.
  5. Set mute groups for hatsDuplicate your hat pad to a second pad. On both, assign Mute Group 1. Now tap closed hat to silence the open one. That is the control.
  6. Layer everything and adjust velocityPlay a note on your controller. Layer 1 and 2 play together. Dial the level of each on the Tune/Mix tab until the stab sits on top of the 808, not buried. You now have a working hip hop voice.
