Module 03 · The machine

Samples: build from recordings

A sample is just a recording. Here's how to load one, trim it to the part you want, and teach it to transpose across your keyboard or stay stuck at one pitch.

The idea

A recording that plays at different pitches

When you record something, it has a speed and a pitch. Play it faster, it sounds higher. Play it slower, it sounds lower. That's a sample.

The MPC lets you load a sample and trim it to the part you like. Then you can transpose it (play it at different pitches) or keep it at one fixed pitch. You can even layer multiple samples across your keyboard so each key plays a different recording, just like a real piano.

This is how drum kits, pianos, and choirs are made on the MPC. A sample and a few settings is all you need.

On the MPC · Samples/Oscs tab

Where you'll find it

Load a sample into a layer. You'll see the waveform with a green Start marker and red End marker. Drag them to trim the region you want to play. Below that, turn Key Track on or off.

Hear it · live in your browser

Drag the trim markers and play

Press play, then drag the green Start or red End marker to trim the sample. The shaded area is what you'll hear. Try the three keys. With Key Track on, they transpose. Turn it off and they all play the same pitch.

Press a key or click:

Sound is generated live. Press a key or click a button, then move the trim markers. Key Track makes it transpose. Turn it off for fixed pitch.

Build it with me · set up your first sample

Five steps to a playable sample

  1. Make a keygroup trackNew track, set its type to Keygroup. This puts you in sample mode.
  2. Load a sample into Layer 1Browse your audio files and pick one. The waveform appears in the Samples/Oscs tab.
  3. Trim it with Start and EndDrag the green Start and red End markers to the part you want. You can see the duration in the display.
  4. Set your root noteTell the MPC what pitch the sample was recorded at. Middle C is the default. This is how transposition knows where to start.
  5. Turn Key Track on, play across the keyboardNow each key you hit transposes the sample to that pitch. Turn Key Track off and every key plays the sample at its original speed.
