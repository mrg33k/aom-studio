Module 04 · The machine

Oscillators: tones drawn from scratch

An oscillator is a machine that makes a tone instead of playing a recording. It draws a shape over and over, very fast. Different shapes sound completely different. By the end you will hear all the main types and pick the right one for what you want to build.

The idea

A shape that vibrates

Imagine a piece of string. When it vibrates, your ear hears a tone. An oscillator is like a tiny invisible string that the MPC draws, over and over, thousands of times a second.

The shape of that string is what you hear. A smooth wave sounds warm and round. A jagged wave sounds bright and buzzy. A bumpy wave sounds broken and weird.

The MPC has nine shapes to pick from. Some are classic (sine, saw, square). Some are weird (FM, ring-mod, noise). Each one is good for something different. Below you will hear them all and learn which one builds what.

Build it with me · make an oscillator layer

Five steps to add tone to your track

  1. Make a keygroup trackNew track, set its type to Keygroup. This is the synth-building mode on the MPC.
  2. Choose Oscillator in Samples/OscsOn the Samples/Oscs tab, change Layer 1 from Sample to Oscillator. You will see the type list.
  3. Pick your shapeFor warm bass or pads, pick Sine. For leads and stabs, pick Saw or Square. For weird sounds, try Pulse or Noise.
  4. Tweak Decay and LevelDecay is how fast the tone fades after you release the key. Level is how loud the oscillator is compared to other layers. Balance them to taste.
  5. Play a note and listenHit a key on the keyboard. That shape is now singing. Stack a second oscillator layer on the same pad to blend shapes and get even richer tones.
