Module 12 · Build lab

Build an evolving pad

A pad is a sound that lives with you. It swells in slowly, changes on its own while you hold it, then fades away. By the end you will build one from a wavetable, hear it morph as you play, and understand every piece that makes it lush.

The idea

A swell that breathes

A pad is not a stab (hit and done). It is a swell (comes up slowly, stays alive, floats out). Imagine opening your hand to a breeze and the air just keeps moving across it. That is a pad.

The magic is in two things. First, a slow amplitude envelope makes the sound grow in and fade out, instead of popping on like a light. Second, a slow LFO wired to the wavetable Position means the tone keeps shifting as you hold it. A pad does not sit still.

You layer it thicker with a second voice (detuned so they shimmer), add a whisper of noise for breath, and run it through a filter that opens and closes gently. That is the whole recipe.

Build it with me · make an evolving pad

Six steps to your own living pad

  1. Make a keygroup trackNew track, set type to Keygroup. This is where you design the sound.
  2. Layer 1: Wavetable oscillatorOn the Samples/Oscs tab, set Layer 1 to Oscillator, pick Wavetable. This is the tone.
  3. Layer 2: Detuned wavetableCopy Layer 1 settings, then raise its Tune value by 12 cents (not semitones, just 12 cents). This shimmer thickens the pad.
  4. Layer 3: Noise for airAdd another layer, set to Oscillator, pick Noise. Lower its Level to about 12 percent. This adds breath without taking over.
  5. Wire the morph: LFO to PositionOpen the Mod Matrix. Route LFO 1 to Wavetable Position. Set the LFO Rate slow (around 0.6 Hz). Now the sound evolves on its own.
  6. Make it swellGo to the Amp envelope. Raise Attack to 1.2 seconds, Release to 2 seconds. Add a subtle low-pass filter. Play a chord and hold it. That is your pad.
