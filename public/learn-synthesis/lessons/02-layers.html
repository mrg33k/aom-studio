Module 02 · The machine

Layers: stack up to 8 sounds inside one keygroup

One keygroup can be many sounds at once. Each one is called a layer. By the end you will hear how layers blend together, and you will build a sound that gets richer and thicker as you toggle them on and off.

The idea

Think of a layer cake

A simple keygroup is one sound: one tone. A layer is just a single sound. But one keygroup can hold up to 8 layers, all stacked on top of each other. Like a cake with up to 8 flavors baked in.

Each layer is a separate sound source. It could be a recording (a sample), or a generated tone (an oscillator). When you play a note, all the layers play at once. The blend sounds richer, warmer, and more alive than any single layer alone.

You choose what each layer does when you play the same note multiple times using Layer Play modes. Play all of them together (Crossfade mode), rotate through them (Cycle), pick a different one based on how hard you hit (Velocity), or roll the dice each time (Random).

Don't mix this up: layers live inside one keygroup (up to 8, all on the same note). Spreading whole keygroups across different keys — a split or a multisample — is a different move, shown on the course map.

On the MPC · Samples/Oscs tab

Where you will see them

On the MPC, every keygroup track shows the Layers strip on the left. You have 8 layer slots, numbered 1 through 8. Each layer slot tells you what the source is (Sample or Oscillator), how loud it is, and if it is turned on or off.

Hear it · toggle layers and listen

Press play, then flip switches

Hit play below, and you will hear a base tone. The buttons are layer toggles. Press Sub and you add a deep bass layer. Press Saw and you hear a bright layer on top. Keep toggling. The more layers you turn on, the thicker and warmer the pad gets. That is the whole magic: more layers means more colors mixed together.

Sound is generated live, no files. Works best with headphones or speakers up.

Build it with me · layer a pad yourself

Five steps to a layered pad

  1. Make a keygroup trackNew track, set its type to Keygroup. This is the instrument-builder mode.
  2. Set Layer 1 to an oscillatorOn the Samples/Oscs tab, switch Layer 1 from Sample to Oscillator. Pick Warm Sine. This is your base.
  3. Turn on Layer 2 and add NoiseScroll down, find Layer 2, set it to Oscillator and choose Noise. Set its level to about 20 percent so it does not drown out Layer 1.
  4. Make both layers swellOn the Amp envelope tab, raise Attack to 1 second and Release to 0.8 seconds. Both layers now fade in smoothly together when you hit the pad.
  5. Hit your pad and hold itOn the MPC keyboard, hit the pad and listen. You will hear the sine and noise blend together. That blend is thicker than either one alone. That is layers working.
