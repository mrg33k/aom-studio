Module 07 · The machine

Envelopes: the shape of a sound over time

Every sound has a life. It starts (attack), it might peak (hold), it settles (decay), and it ends (release). You control all four with one word: envelopes. By the end you will hear how they shape a pluck into a pad, and you will build your first sound from scratch.

The idea

How a sound turns on and off

Imagine a light switch. You flip it on. It glows instantly, or it takes a second to warm up (attack). Once it is hot, does it stay bright, or fade to a comfortable level (decay and sustain). When you flip it off, does it go dark right away, or does it fade out slowly (release). That whole journey is the envelope.

An envelope is the volume shape of a sound across time. It lives in six parts: Delay (wait), Attack (rise), Hold (peak), Decay (fall), Sustain (hold), Release (fade after you let go). Together they make the letters DAHDSR.

Every sound on the MPC has four separate envelopes running at once: Amp (volume, the main one), Filter (brightness), Pitch (tune), and Aux (a spare). You set them once and they play the same way every time you hit a pad.

On the MPC · Envelopes tab

Where you will find it

Open any keygroup, go to the Envelopes tab. You see four sub-tabs: Amp, Filter, Pitch, Aux. Choose Amp. The screen shows the envelope graph with six labeled points, and six knobs to move them.

Hear it · live in your browser

Drag Attack and Release, hear it change

Press play. You hear a tone. The red line on the left is the envelope shape. Now drag Attack slow. The tone fades in instead of starting sharp. Then drag Release long. The tone lingers when you let go. Try the Pluck and Pad presets to hear the extreme difference the envelope makes to the same tone.

Sound is generated live, no files. Works best with headphones or speakers up.

Build it with me · make a plucked string

Five steps to your first synth sound

  1. Make a keygroup trackNew track, set its type to Keygroup. This unlocks the synth engine.
  2. Set a layer to Oscillator, choose SawOn the Samples/Oscs tab, switch Layer 1 from Sample to Oscillator, then pick Saw from the list. Sawtooth is the workhorse.
  3. Open the Amp envelopeGo to the Envelopes tab, choose the Amp sub-tab. You see the six knobs you learned above.
  4. Make it pluckSet Attack to 0.02 (instant), Decay to 0.15, Sustain to 0. Let go of the pad and the sound stops right away. That is a pluck.
  5. Change it to a padNow set Attack to 1.0 (slow), Decay to 0.1, Sustain to 0.8, Release to 2.0. Hold the pad. It swells in, holds, fades out slow. That is a pad.
