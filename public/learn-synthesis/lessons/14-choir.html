Module 14 · Build lab

Build a choir

A choir is many voices singing the same note at slightly different times and pitches. By the end you will layer voices together, give them a slow swell, add gentle movement, and hear the magic that makes a pad sound alive.

The idea

Many voices in one sound

Imagine 4 people singing the exact same note. They will never sing it identically. Their pitches drift. They breathe at different times. That tiny difference makes the sound warm and thick.

A choir patch stacks multiple voices together. Each one is almost the same, but not quite. On the MPC you can use samples (real voices) or warm sine oscillators stacked to create that same choir feeling. Give them a slow swell (slow attack and release) and the whole thing breathes. Add a gentle pitch wiggle and it sounds alive instead of static.

That warmth is the whole point. Alone, one voice is thin. Together, 4 or 6 voices become something bigger than themselves.

On the MPC · Samples/Oscs tab

Stacking layers into a choir

A choir patch uses multiple layers. Each layer is an oscillator (we use Warm Sine) or a sampled voice. Set them slightly different pitches in the Tune/Mix tab, spread them across stereo in the Pan knob, and balance their levels. The magic is the difference between them.

Hear it · live in your browser

Hold a chord and feel it swell

Press the Hold Chord button to hear the patch. A chord held down lets you hear the swell and the movement. Use Voice Count to add and remove voices. One voice sounds thin. Three voices sound full. Six voices are lush and thick. That progression is the teaching.

Sound is generated live, no files. Hold the chord to hear the swell and how voices fill it in.

Build it with me · make a choir patch

Five steps to your own choir

  1. Make a keygroup trackNew track, set its type to Keygroup. This unlocks the sound design tools.
  2. Create 4 layers, all Warm Sine oscillatorsOn Samples/Oscs, add 4 layers. Set each to Oscillator, then Warm Sine. They all play the same note, but voices matter now.
  3. Detune them slightly in the Tune/Mix tabLayer 1 stays at 0. Layer 2 goes to +5 cents. Layer 3 goes to +8 cents. Layer 4 goes to -3 cents. Tiny differences. Huge effect.
  4. Spread them in stereo with PanLayer 1 center. Layer 2 left. Layer 3 right. Layer 4 slightly left. The voices feel wider and bigger now.
  5. Make it swell with the Amp envelopeOn the Amp tab, set Attack to 1.5 seconds. Set Release to 2 seconds. Now when you hit a note it fades in, holds, and fades out like a real breath. Press a chord and hold it. That is your finished choir.
