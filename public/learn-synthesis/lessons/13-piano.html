Module 13 · Build lab

Build a piano with velocity layers

A real piano sounds different when you play it soft versus hard. Hit harder and the recording changes. This is the sampler's superpower. By the end you'll have layered samples that switch based on how hard you play, and you'll hear why real instruments feel alive.

The idea

Three pianos hiding in one

Imagine recording a piano three times: once playing very softly, once at medium volume, and once banging the keys hard. The soft one sounds mellow and dark. The medium one is normal. The hard one is bright and aggressive. All three are piano, but each has its own tone.

Now hide all three inside one keygroup. Tell the MPC: play the soft recording when you hit the pad gently, play the medium one for normal hits, and play the hard one when you play aggressively. That's called velocity layers. Every layer sits in a velocity range. Hit softer or harder and a different layer wakes up. From the outside, it looks like one piano. Inside, it's three timbres taking turns.

That's why sampled pianos feel real. The machine does exactly what a real player does: change tone based on how hard you touch the keys.

On the MPC · Samples/Oscs tab

Where you'll set it up

Pick the Samples/Oscs tab, then the Velocity sub-tab. Each layer gets a velocity range (Vel Start and Vel End), a root note (the pitch sample), and a reference amplitude level. You'll also check Key Track to let the pitch follow your notes across the keyboard.

Play soft, medium, and hard

Click a velocity button or drag the slider to change how hard the piano is being played. Each step switches the tone. Soft is mellow. Medium is balanced. Hard is bright and percussive. Listen for the timbral shift as you move between layers. That's what makes a sampled piano feel real.

Build it with me · set up velocity layers

Six steps to a realistic piano

  1. Make a keygroup trackNew track, set type to Keygroup. This is your instrument builder.
  2. Load or record three piano samplesGet or record a C3 piano note at three dynamics: soft, medium, and hard. Trim them to the same length so they feel aligned.
  3. Assign each to a layer and set velocity rangesLayer 1 gets the soft sample, Vel Start 0 to Vel End 42. Layer 2 is medium, 43 to 85. Layer 3 is hard, 86 to 127. Check the Velocity tab.
  4. Set root notes and turn on Key TrackAll three layers: root note is C3. In the Keygroup Global tab, turn Key Track ON so the pitch follows your notes.
  5. Dial in the amp envelopeOn the Amp tab, set Attack to around 0.1 seconds for piano realism. Leave Release at a natural tail (around 1 second). Piano rings out.
  6. Play and listen to layer switchesHold one note and tap gently, then harder. You'll hear the tone shift as layers switch. Hit all three velocity zones and feel the realism.
