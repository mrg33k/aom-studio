Module 06 · The machine

Filters: the tone shaper

Filters are sunglasses for sound. They let you dial any brightness level you want. By the end you'll hear a filter sweep, understand cutoff and resonance, and build a sound that opens like a flower.

The idea

It's sunglasses for your tone

A normal oscillator plays one brightness forever. A filter takes that tone and darkens it. You pick how much to darken it with the cutoff knob.

Imagine sunglasses that go from clear to dark. Cutoff all the way up means no tint, every note sounds full and bright. Cutoff down means the sunglasses are dark, every note sounds muffled. In the middle you get a sweet spot.

Resonance is the second knob. Low resonance means the filter darkens smoothly. High resonance adds a whistle right at the cutoff point. So you get a pokey, vocal quality that stands out.

That's 90 percent of what filters do. The MPC runs two at once, stacked or in parallel, so you can layer sounds even richer. And one more trick: keytrack makes the filter follow the note you play so every pitch sounds as bright as the one before.

On the MPC · Filters tab

Where you'll find it

Every keygroup has two filters by default. You can run them in Serial mode (one into the other, for deep color changes) or Parallel (side by side, for blending two tones). The screen shows all the knobs: Cutoff, Resonance, Output, and Env (how the filter envelope opens it).

Hear it · live in your browser

Drag Cutoff and listen to the tone shift

Press play and drag the Cutoff slider all the way down. The synth turns dark and muffled. Drag it back up and the brightness returns. Now try Resonance with cutoff in the middle. You are adding a whistle, a vocal peak that makes the filter obvious. This is how filters make music alive.

Sound is generated live, no files. Works best with headphones or speakers up.

Build it with me · create a filter sweep

Five steps to a filter that opens like a flower

  1. Make a keygroup trackNew track, set its type to Keygroup. This is the instrument-builder mode.
  2. Set a layer to Oscillator, pick SawtoothOn the Samples/Oscs tab, switch Layer 1 from Sample to Oscillator. Saw has a bright, buzzy quality that makes filter movement obvious.
  3. Adjust the Amp envelope for a padOn the Amp envelope, raise Attack to 0.5 seconds and Release to 1 second. This gives the note time to breathe.
  4. Wire the filter envelope to CutoffOn the Filters tab, set the Env knob to around 80. This means the filter envelope opens the cutoff when you play. Play a note and hold it, the tone should bloom from dark to bright over time.
  5. Add resonance for characterRaise the Resonance knob to 8 or 10. The filter sweep now has a vocal, peaked quality. Play a chord and hold it. That is a finished filter sweep pad.
