01

Creative Intent

Wedding films work because they are devotion stories — love captured with reverence. Applied to ISA, this same grammar lets us tell a decade-long mission as a love story pointed at energy, the planet, the future. Hunter is the scientist and the spine. Skylar arrives to shift the energy and name the uncomfortable truth. The footage is cinematic, the grade is warm-editorial, and the cadence breathes. This is not a corporate explainer. It is a devotion film.

Every shot should feel like a still you'd frame. Every cut should feel earned. When we say epic, we mean slower holds, bigger music, fewer cuts — not machine-gun b-roll.

02

Style DNA (from the wedding reference)

  • Editorial portraits, not establishing shots. The reference opens with an intimate fashion-editorial portrait of the couple. Our equivalent: Hunter and Skylar shot like founders posed for a magazine cover. Tight, shallow DOF, slightly off-axis, direct gaze when it matters.
  • Speaker portraits over talking heads. When a founder speaks, the frame is intentional. Background bokeh, shoulder-up, stillness. Not documentary "interview" energy — magazine-cover energy.
  • Detail shots that mean something. In the wedding ref, a chair with a memorial card says more than any voiceover. For ISA: a hand on the V3 box, a line in an engineer's notebook, a waveform on an oscilloscope. Objects that testify.
  • Celebration beat. Reference has an emotional dance moment (bride hands to face). Our equivalent is the proof moment — the decade of work paying off. Team glances, prototype humming, a smile we don't explain.
  • Wide establishing late, not early. The reference withholds the wide of the venue until near the end (~1:00). We do the same with the Sedona lab — the reveal lands after the breakthrough, not before.
  • Titles as moments of stillness. The reference end card is 90% negative space. Our open and close titles do the same. Black frame, small serif, breathe.

03

Color & Grade

  • Shadows: warm teal. Not blue. Not black. Teal that remembers gold.
  • Midtones: warm amber, skin pushed slightly toward gold. Hunter and Skylar should feel lit from within.
  • Highlights: soft roll-off. Never clipped. Sedona's natural light does the work — don't fight it.
  • Contrast: lifted blacks. Filmic, not digital. Think A24 documentary, not Netflix corporate.
  • Saturation: selective. Skin and device metals stay rich. Background foliage and walls desaturate slightly.
  • Grain: light 35mm grain over the entire film. Sells the cinematic.

04

Music Direction

  • Arc: contemplative piano open → strings enter at 0:32 ("the truth") → build through 1:18 → hold through the discovery → peak at 1:55 ("we've proven") → sustain through the product → resolve at the end card.
  • Instrumentation: felt piano, cello, sustained strings, soft timpani hit or heartbeat pulse under the proof moment. Avoid synthy "corporate tech" trailer pads.
  • BPM: ~70-80. Slow enough to breathe, fast enough to build.
  • Reference composers (do not license without cover): Max Richter ("On the Nature of Daylight"), Jóhann Jóhannsson ("The Theory of Everything"), Hans Zimmer ("Time" from Inception), Hammock, Ólafur Arnalds, Nils Frahm.
  • First test: try HERITAGE and STAND from the existing Adobe Stock library on SS8TB. Most likely to carry the emotional build.
  • If those don't hit: jump to Envato Elements Curated Searches below. Every link is a pre-filtered result page — click to audition in browser.

06

Pacing Rules

  • Average cut: 2.5-4s. Match the wedding reference cadence (81s / ~30 beats = 2.7s avg).
  • Longest holds: 5-6s. Reserved for emotional peaks — the proof, the discovery reveal, the end card.
  • Shortest cuts: 2s. Reserved for attribute reveals and montage stacks.
  • One breath per section. Don't stack talking-head shots back to back. Cut to detail/b-roll between founder beats.
  • When narration is powerful, let it ride on a longer hold. Don't chop the line to prove you can cut.

07

Titles & Text Treatment

  • Font: Syne (headers) or a thin serif like Optima/Didot. Letter-spaced, small, never bold-shouty.
  • Open card: "ISA" centered on black. Below: "A FILM ON POWER REIMAGINED" in small caps, wide kerning. 3 seconds.
  • Section titles (if any): None. Let the narration mark the sections.
  • Attribute overlays (Section 4): bottom-center, small caps, wide tracking. ALWAYS ON  ·  DECENTRALIZED  ·  SCALABLE  ·  SUSTAINABLE.
  • Application overlays (Section 7): same treatment. HOMES  ·  COMMUNITIES  ·  DATA CENTERS.
  • End card: Black frame. "ISA" centered. Below: "POWER REIMAGINED" small caps, wide tracking. 2 seconds. Match the wedding ref's "THE WEDDING OF JUNELLE + NOAH" energy exactly.

AI Gap Shots — Paired Prompts

Every gap shot gets two paired prompts: nano banana generates a still frame that locks the tone, composition, and lighting. Seedance then takes that still as input and animates it the way we imagined. The still is the tone reference. The motion prompt turns it into the shot.

Pipeline: generate still in nano banana at 16:9, 3840×2160 or larger → feed to Seedance image-to-video at highest quality → 4-6s clip → drop into Resolve.
