Prepared: May 28, 2026

The Operator Who Changed The Profile

William Host is not a small Pittsburgh marketing agency owner looking to bolt AI onto his existing clients. He is a multi-role operator embedded at the senior level of a $30M+ Inc. 5000 company, running acquisition and business development for one of the fastest-growing fractional services platforms in professional services.

The research that came back on Will required a full revision. What looked like a straightforward agency acquisition conversation is something altogether different — and far more interesting.

Who Is Will Host — The Full Stack

Will operates simultaneously across three professional roles, each substantial in its own right:

Role Company Nature
Owner & CEO Andocia Creative Agency Founder-owned marketing agency, Oakmont, PA
Director of Acquisitions How To MANAGE a Small Law Firm (HTM) Senior business development at $30M+ company

How To MANAGE a Small Law Firm — The Game-Changing Piece

HTM is the load-bearing element that transforms the entire deal profile.

Key Metric

How To MANAGE a Small Law Firm: $30M+ annual revenue, 600+ active members, operating on a three-year waitlist, Inc. 5000 honoree every year since 2015 (9+ consecutive years).

What It Does

Founded in 2009 by RJon Robins, HTM is a membership-based business coaching and outsourced C-suite services platform for solo and small law firm owners. Members get fractional CEO, COO, CFO, and CMO services — the operational infrastructure that law firm owners don't have in-house.

The Pricing & Scale

  • $1,500–$2,000/month per member — 600+ members paying this = $900K–$1.2M/month recurring revenue
  • $30M+ annual revenue stated by founder RJon Robins; 8,000% growth since 2009
  • Three-year waitlist — unmet demand; the model can't scale with humans alone
  • Expansion phase — as of 2024, partnering with Scaling Up to serve $10M–$50M+ firms; moving upmarket

How To MANAGE: $30M+ company on the Inc. 5000 list 9+ consecutive years

Will's Role — Director of Acquisitions at HTM

Will is listed on the official HTM team page (howtomanageasmalllawfirm.com/our-team) alongside 80+ other staff members. At a company on a three-year waitlist actively expanding, "Director of Acquisitions" translates to: senior business development, converting prospects into paying members, managing the sales pipeline for 600+ firms.

He was brought in as a marketing authority (Andocia's expertise) for HTM's law firm owner base, and the relationship evolved into a formal staff role. The connection is deep, not transactional.

Will's primary job function is acquiring new clients into a high-growth, high-touch service platform. He understands demand generation, sales pipeline, and how to move prospects from interest to paying membership at scale.

This is a network of people who are already paying for premium services, understand ROI on operational and marketing improvement, and are actively managed by a coaching company that has trained them to spend money on business infrastructure.

The revised assessment, with HTM in the picture, is substantively different.

600+ law firm owners actively buying operational and marketing services

The Strategic Fit with AOM

The most interesting play: AOM becomes the AI execution layer powering HTM's fractional CMO offering at scale.

  • HTM's fractional CMO program serves approximately 25% of its member base — that's roughly 150 law firms already paying for outsourced marketing leadership
  • AOM's agent platform could be positioned as the technology substrate underneath HTM's service model at scale — turning human fractional CMO work into AI-augmented, higher-throughput execution
  • Law firm owners as early adopters — entrepreneurs who understand ROI, already accustomed to paying for premium services, and actively looking for competitive advantages
  • Will understands both sides — he's lived the agency experience (Andocia) and the professional services coaching model (HTM). He can translate between both worlds
The Capacity Problem HTM Faces

A three-year waitlist means demand exceeds supply. Human-powered fractional services hit a ceiling. AI-augmented service delivery (powered by AOM's agents) is the obvious answer to that capacity bottleneck — and Will knows it.

Critical Unknowns for the Deal Conversation

The conversation with Will needs clarity on structure from the start. Several different deals are possible, and each one changes the leverage and path forward:

  • Is he exploring an acquisition of AOM? Will/Andocia buys AOM outright
  • Is he pursuing a merger? Andocia merges with AOM; combined entity serves his network
  • Is he looking to license AOM's platform for HTM clients? AOM stays independent; HTM becomes a major channel partner
  • Is this a personal investment play? Will individually invests in AOM and leverages his network for distribution

The structure changes everything. Knowing which deal he's really exploring is the first move.

His compensation at HTM as Director of Acquisitions at a $30M company, plus his CEO role at Andocia, plus his CBO role at Beratung, plus real estate ownership — he has material financial resources and decision-making authority.

Bottom Line

Will Host is not a small Pittsburgh agency owner. He is a multi-role operator with a formal senior position at a $30M+ Inc. 5000 company that serves 600+ law firm owners on a subscription model. His network represents a concentrated pool of high-earning professional services entrepreneurs who are active buyers of operational and marketing improvement — and he has the professional services expertise to help position AOM's technology into that ecosystem.

The most valuable outcome: positioning AOM as the AI platform layer underneath HTM's fractional CMO offering, transforming 150 law firms from human-served to AI-augmented execution, and building a scalable revenue model around that partnership.

The next conversation needs to start with one clear question: What is he actually trying to do? Is this Andocia looking to acquire AI capability? Is this HTM looking to embed AOM's platform into their member services? Is this a personal investment and distribution play? The answer changes the structure and leverage entirely.
