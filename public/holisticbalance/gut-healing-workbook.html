Your Personal Practice Workbook

This workbook is a companion to the course — not a summary of it. Use it between modules to go deeper, track what's actually happening in your body, and build the habits that stick. Bring it back to each session with your notes already in it.

How to Use This Workbook

Work through each section after completing the corresponding course module — or return to it whenever something comes up. There are no right answers. Write honestly, even if what you notice is messy or contradictory.

The tracker on page 4 is designed for Week 1, but you can repeat it on paper for any week you want to observe more closely. The recipes are yours to try, adapt, and keep.

Module 1 Companion

Your Gut Baseline

Seeing where you actually are — before you change anything

Before any protocol makes sense, you need a clear picture of your starting point. This is the map you'll navigate from.

Deeper Reflection

Think back 5 years. How has your relationship with your digestion changed? What events — stress, travel, illness, medication, diet shifts — coincide with how things feel now?

Body Mapping

Where do you feel discomfort most often — upper abdomen, lower abdomen, chest, throat? Does it come with meals, between meals, or at certain times of day? Be as specific as you can.

The Stress Connection

How does stress show up in your gut? Think of a specific stressful situation from the past month — what happened in your body during and after?

What Have You Already Tried?

List any diets, supplements, or approaches you've tried for gut health. For each one, what happened? What worked even a little — and what didn't?

Module 2 Companion

Food as Medicine

Making the connection between what you eat and how you feel

Your Honest Eating Pattern

Write out what you actually ate yesterday — not what you think you should have eaten. Include when you ate, how hungry you were, and what you were doing or feeling at the time.

Pattern Recognition

Are there foods you suspect don't agree with you — but you haven't been able to confirm or give up? What stops you from eliminating them even temporarily?

The Gut-Brain Meal

Describe the last time you ate while anxious, rushed, or distracted. What did you eat, and how did your body respond in the hours after? What would eating that same meal slowly and calmly feel like?

One Honest Change

If you were going to make one dietary change this week — just one, that feels genuinely doable — what would it be? Why this one, and not another?

Module 3 Companion

Fermentation & Probiotic Foods

Bringing live cultures into your daily routine

Your Fermented Food History

What fermented foods have you eaten regularly in the past — even without knowing they were fermented? Which did you enjoy, and which felt strange or foreign? What's your earliest memory of fermented food?

The Introduction Plan

Choose one fermented food to add this week. Write down specifically when in your day you'll eat it, how much you'll start with, and what you'll pair it with. Be specific enough that you can follow through without re-deciding each morning.

Try This Recipe

Simple Miso Soup

5 minutes · Serves 1 · Probiotic-rich
Ingredients
  • 1 cup hot water (not boiling)
  • 1 tbsp white or yellow miso paste
  • ½ tsp dried wakame seaweed
  • 2–3 cubes soft silken tofu
  • 1 spring onion, thinly sliced
  • Optional: a few drops of sesame oil
Method
  1. Heat water until steaming but not boiling — around 70°C / 160°F.
  2. Place miso paste in your bowl and add a small splash of hot water. Stir until fully dissolved.
  3. Soak wakame in a little water for 2 minutes until rehydrated. Drain and add to bowl.
  4. Add tofu, pour over remaining hot water, top with spring onion.
  5. Taste before adding anything — miso is already salty and complex.
Miso is a live fermented food — boiling destroys the beneficial bacteria. Keep the water below boiling, and you preserve the probiotic benefit. White miso is mildest; red miso is bolder and slightly higher in probiotics.
After Trying It

After you've tried the miso soup (or another fermented food this week) — what did you notice? Any gut responses, taste reactions, or shifts in how you felt in the hours that followed?

Module 4 Companion

Reducing Inflammation

Finding what inflames your system — and what calms it

Your Inflammation Signals

Beyond digestion — where else do you feel inflammation in your body? Joint stiffness, skin flares, brain fog, fatigue, mood crashes? When do these tend to cluster? What tends to come before them?

The Sleep-Gut Loop

Honestly describe your sleep right now — hours, quality, how you feel on waking. Do you notice any connection between nights you sleep poorly and how your gut behaves the next day?

Try This Recipe

Golden Gut Smoothie

5 minutes · Serves 1 · Anti-inflammatory
Ingredients
  • 1 cup unsweetened almond or oat milk
  • ½ frozen banana (for creaminess)
  • ½ tsp ground turmeric
  • ¼ tsp ground ginger (or 1 tsp fresh)
  • A small pinch of black pepper
  • 1 tsp raw honey or maple syrup
  • Optional: 1 tbsp ground flaxseed
Method
  1. Add all ingredients to a blender.
  2. Blend on high for 30–45 seconds until completely smooth.
  3. Taste — adjust honey or ginger to your preference.
  4. Drink immediately, or refrigerate up to 4 hours (shake before drinking).
Black pepper is not optional — it contains piperine, which increases curcumin absorption from turmeric by up to 2000%. Even a pinch makes a meaningful difference. The banana adds prebiotic fibre that feeds your gut bacteria.
Building Your Anti-Inflammatory Day

Design one ideal anti-inflammatory day for yourself — meals, movement, sleep timing, stress breaks. Don't aim for perfection; aim for something you could actually do next Tuesday. What would change, and what stays the same?

Module 5 Companion

Building Your Protocol

Turning what you've learned into something you'll actually keep

The best protocol is the one you'll sustain for months, not the most thorough one you'll abandon in a week. Design for your real life, not your ideal life.

What You're Taking Forward

From everything across this course — the ideas, the practices, the moments of recognition — which three things feel most relevant to your life right now? Not the three most impressive. The three that most fit.

What You'll Stop

Is there one habit, food, or pattern you're ready to reduce or let go of — at least for a defined period to see how your body responds? What's your honest plan for that?

Anticipating Resistance

You know yourself. What will make you fall off the protocol — social pressure, busy weeks, cost, boredom? For each one, write a specific plan for what you'll do instead of quitting.

Your 30-Day Commitment

In your own words, write a short commitment to yourself — what you'll try, for how long, and what you'll measure. Not a contract. Just a clear statement you can return to when things get hard.
